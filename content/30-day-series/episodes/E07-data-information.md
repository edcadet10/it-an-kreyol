# E07 — Done vin enfòmasyon

## Learning objective

The learner can explain that data are recorded values/symbols and that context and processing can turn them into useful information.

## Audience problem

“Data” is often treated only as a mobile-plan allowance, hiding its broader role in files, apps, decisions, privacy, and IT work.

## Draft beats

- **Hook draft:** “`72, 75, 79`—san kontèks, chif sa yo pa di w anpil.”
- Add labels such as “tanperati chak jou” or a fictional score to show context.
- Explain that text, images, sound, and measurements can all be represented as data.
- Processing/organization can make data useful, but incorrect context or bad data can mislead.
- Mention mobile data as one use/transfer context, not the definition of all data.

## Visual

Unlabeled values transform into a tiny labeled table/chart. Use invented non-personal values.

## Knowledge check / CTA

“Ki sa ki manke pou twa chif yo tounen enfòmasyon itil: plis koulè, oswa kontèks?”

## Evidence

- [Microsoft Learn — Exploring basic computer concepts](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/training/paths/explore-basic-computer-concepts/) as curriculum context
- [NIST CSRC glossary — data](https://csrc.nist.gov/glossary/term/data) — use the representation/processing definitions in their cited context
- [NIST information-quality standards](https://www.nist.gov/director/nist-information-quality-standards) — information and the importance of context/objectivity

## Review flags

- NIST lists several contextual definitions; do not present the data/information distinction as one universally fixed boundary.
- Avoid claiming data becomes objectively meaningful merely because it has a label.
