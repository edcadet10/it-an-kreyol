# E22 — Modpas long, inik, epi yon password manager

## Learning objective

The learner can explain why important accounts need long, unique passwords and why a reputable password manager reduces the burden of creating and storing them.

## Audience problem

Password reuse lets one stolen credential threaten many accounts, while old composition folklore encourages short predictable substitutions.

## Draft beats

- **Hook draft:** “Si yon sèl modpas ouvri tout account ou yo, yon sèl vòl ka louvri tout pòt yo.”
- Prioritize length and uniqueness; NIST consumer guidance recommends at least 15 characters where a password is used/allowed.
- A password manager can generate and store different passwords for each service.
- Protect the manager itself with its supported strong authentication/MFA and recovery plan.
- Passwords are not phishing-resistant; this episode does not promise that long passwords stop every attack.

## Visual

One reused key opens five doors, then separate generated keys stored in a vault. Never display a real or reusable example password.

## Knowledge check / CTA

“Ki pi bon: yon modpas konplike ou repete tout kote, oswa modpas long epi diferan pou chak account?”

## Evidence

- [NIST — How Do I Create a Good Password?](https://www.nist.gov/cybersecurity-and-privacy/how-do-i-create-good-password)
- [NIST SP 800-63B-4 — Password authenticators](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b.html#passwords)

## Review flags

- Recheck current NIST text at script approval.
- Do not name a “best” commercial manager without a separate current comparison and test.
