# E24 — Rekonèt phishing

## Learning objective

The learner can pause on an unexpected request, avoid its link/attachment, and verify through a phone number, app, bookmark, or website already known to be genuine.

## Audience problem

Modern phishing can use good spelling, familiar logos, urgency, and personal context, so a checklist based only on obvious errors is weak.

## Draft beats

- **Hook draft:** “Mesaj la di account ou ap fèmen nan 10 minit. Se ijans lan menm ki vle fè w sispann reflechi.”
- Unexpected requests for login, payment, personal data, attachment opening, or MFA codes deserve a pause.
- Do not use the contact information or link supplied in the suspicious message to verify itself.
- Open the known app/site or call a known number independently.
- Report using platform/organization channels and delete only after any needed reporting/evidence step.

## Visual

Fictional message with generic brand, invented data, and no live link. Mark urgency, unexpected request, and verification path.

## Knowledge check / CTA

“Ki kote ou dwe jwenn nimewo pou verifye mesaj bank la: nan menm mesaj la, oswa nan yon sous ou te deja konnen?”

## Evidence

- [FTC — How to Recognize and Avoid Phishing Scams](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/how-recognize-avoid-phishing-scams)
- [NIST — Protecting Your Small Business: Phishing](https://www.nist.gov/itl/smallbusinesscyber/guidance-topic/phishing)

## Review flags

- Security reviewer approval required.
- Do not say spelling mistakes are required; AI-assisted scams may look polished.
