## 1. Purpose and opportunity

The project will create clear, practical information-technology lessons in Haitian Creole for people who may encounter most technical education in English or French. It will also demonstrate disciplined project-management practice through a public GitHub repository.

The opportunity has two parts:

1. Give Haitian Creole-speaking beginners a low-friction path into digital literacy and IT.
2. Build a verifiable portfolio showing how an idea moves through initiation, planning, delivery, control, evaluation, and closeout or continuation.

The project does **not** assume that TikTok demand, topic fit, or a daily creator cadence is already validated. The 30-day release is the first deliberate market and operating test.

## 2. Product goal

Build a trusted Haitian Creole learning channel that helps beginners understand, use, troubleshoot, and protect everyday technology, while creating a path toward deeper IT learning and careers.

## 3. First-release objectives

1. **Prepare:** Before Day 1, define all 30 learning objectives and have at least seven creator-reviewed videos ready to post.
2. **Deliver:** Publish one numbered video on each of 30 consecutive calendar days.
3. **Protect quality:** Require every published video to pass the technical, language, accessibility, safety, and platform checks in the quality plan.
4. **Preserve the experiment:** During Days 1–30, record only whether the planned episode was posted. Do not collect audience, workflow, quality, timing, buffer, or exception data. Critical factual, privacy, copyright, or safety issues may always trigger action.
5. **Evaluate:** Starting after video 30, collect comparable evidence, review comments and analytics, document lessons, and make a continue/adapt/pause decision.
6. **Teach project management:** Maintain current baselines, registers, decision records, retrospectives, and final lessons in the repository.

These are project thresholds selected for this release. They are not universal TikTok benchmarks.

## 4. High-level requirements

- Spoken instruction is primarily Haitian Creole.
- English industry terms appear on screen when useful for search, certification, interfaces, or employment.
- Each video teaches one testable idea and ends with a recall prompt, application prompt, or question.
- Technical claims cite at least one credible source in the episode brief.
- Security demonstrations use test accounts, invented data, and sanitized screens.
- Captions are manually reviewed; auto-generated captions are never trusted without review.
- The first 30 posts are visibly numbered and connected as a series.
- Raw video, account credentials, participant identities, and private analytics exports stay out of the public repository.
- Project-management artifacts remain readable to people who are learning the discipline.

## 5. Scope

### In scope for Release 1

- Audience discovery sufficient to create a beginner curriculum
- A 30-episode foundational IT curriculum
- Research, scripting, recording, editing, quality review, captions, and publication
- Basic profile and series identity
- Comment moderation and factual corrections
- Project governance, schedule, risk, quality, resource, and communication controls
- Post-challenge analytics and qualitative review
- Public portfolio documentation in GitHub

### Out of scope for Release 1

- Paid promotion or advertising
- Monetization targets
- Sponsorships or brand deals
- A formal certification or accredited course
- Personalized device repair, account recovery, or cybersecurity incident response
- Advanced coding, cloud certification, or enterprise administration courses
- A custom website, mobile app, or learning-management system
- Purchasing equipment before a documented need and budget decision
- Claiming learning outcomes from views or likes alone

See the controlled [scope and work breakdown structure](03-scope-and-wbs.md).

## 7. Constraints and assumptions

### Constraints

- The release cadence is one video per calendar day for 30 days.
- During Days 1–30, the only recorded project datum is whether the planned episode was posted.
- Day 1 is October 12, 2026; Day 30 is November 10, 2026.
- Creator capacity is limited to 8–12 hours per week.
- The creator is provisionally treated as a solo core team.
- TikTok is a third-party platform whose features and distribution may change.
- The repository is public, so confidential or licensed source material cannot be stored there.

### Assumptions requiring confirmation

- The selected audience boundary—Haitian Creole-speaking beginners aged roughly 16+ in Haiti and the diaspora—will prove useful enough to continue after Release 1.
- The confirmed 8–12 hours per week can support the fixed date after a sample forecast and contingency are applied.
- The Nikon Z50, laptop, professional lighting, and background can meet visual needs; audio, editing, storage, and backup remain to be tested.
- Structured creator self-review can catch sufficient Kreyòl, technical, accessibility, privacy, and safety defects without independent approval.
- The repo is intended to be public under the GitHub account `edcadet10`.

All assumptions are tracked in [the assumptions and decisions log](11-assumptions-and-decisions.md).

## 9. Governance and authority

- The sponsor/product owner owns the vision, approves baselines, accepts risks, and has final publish authority.
- The project manager maintains the plan and escalates material variance.
- The AI assistant may research, draft, structure, check, and explain; it cannot approve accuracy, language, privacy, or publication on behalf of a human.
- For Release 1, the creator performs separate Kreyòl/language and IT/source review passes and gives final approval.
- No independent reviewer is assigned. That accepted choice remains a high residual risk rather than being represented as independent verification.
- Any person may stop publication for a suspected critical privacy, security, copyright, or safety issue.

A material change to audience, release objective, 30-day cadence, measurement blackout, budget ceiling, or safety rules requires a decision-log entry and sponsor approval.

## 10. Initial risks

The leading threats are inaccurate terminology, technical misinformation, creator self-review blind spots, an unvalidated 8–12-hour capacity forecast, the strict no-tracking blackout, insufficient content buffer, platform dependence, and accidental exposure of private data. Owners and responses are recorded in the [risk register](06-risk-register.md).

## 11. Benefits and success interpretation

Delivery success means producing and publishing the agreed series safely and consistently. Product success means credible evidence that the material is useful and understandable to the intended audience. Portfolio success means the repository honestly shows decisions, tradeoffs, results, and lessons—including weak or negative results.

Completing 30 posts alone does not prove audience fit. High view counts alone do not prove learning. Those distinctions prevent activity and popularity from being mistaken for value.

## PM lesson: what a charter does

A project charter authorizes a project at a useful level of detail. It connects the work to a reason, names authority, defines boundaries, and makes uncertainty visible. It is not the full plan. Here, the charter says what Release 1 must accomplish; the remaining documents explain how the team will control the work.
